# Incoming mail protection

All **new** received email uses one pipeline, whether received through verified SMIP, managed Postfix, or the signed inbound-relay API. A signed SMIP receipt means durable acceptance, including quarantine. It does not certify content safety. Raw MIME and attachments remain private.

In **Settings → Mail → Incoming mail protection**, workspace owners/admins can change AI screening, unknown-sender approval, sender/domain hourly limits and the quarantine threshold. Members can inspect warnings and report/retract their own spam complaints; approval, blocking, release and rescan require workspace administration. The message's protection panel uses the same actions on desktop and phone. Held mail is in **Spam**, with a reason and individual AI/antivirus states.

## Policy and reputation

Defaults are 60 messages per sender/hour, 600 per domain/hour, risk threshold 70/100, AI screening enabled, and unknown-sender approval disabled. Domain limits and complaint reputation pool subdomains under the registrable parent domain. These limits are workspace-wide UTC-hour buckets, independent of the destination mailbox. Duplicate deliveries return the original receipt/item before charging another allowance. Rate-limited deliveries return 429 without storing a second message. Postfix retains its queue and retries; SMIP honors bounded Retry-After without provider fallback. Other inbound adapters must retain and retry the same delivery ID.

Verified SMIP and SMTP/relay identities have separate rate/reputation/approval namespaces. Relay HMAC authenticates the relay, **not** a claimed From address; spoofed relay mail cannot inherit a verified SMIP sender's approval. These namespaces are fixed at intake and are unchanged by SPF/DKIM/DMARC (see below). These counters are not network-edge denial-of-service protection; rotating spoofed addresses/domains still require edge limits.

Reputation is private to each workspace. Reports are unique per actor/message, and repeated reports on the same message count once toward reputation. Three distinct reported messages produce poor sender reputation; ten reported messages across an authenticated-class/domain produce poor domain reputation. Poor reputation contributes risk 80. An explicit block contributes risk 100. Retracting feedback removes only the current actor's complaint. Reports and decisions are audited; deleting an email removes its associated complaint records. There is no global reputation service or automatic expiry/decay in this version.

When unknown-sender approval is enabled, a message is known only when its own sender is verified and the address is approved: a trusted identity whose message is delivered over SMIP or passes SPF/DKIM/DMARC (see [Trusted senders must authenticate](#trusted-senders-must-authenticate)), or a verified SMIP sender exactly matching a current workspace contact/member. An address in Contacts alone does not authenticate relay mail. **Approve sender** marks the address trusted for later mail that authenticates, and approves the message it was used on; it does not skip scanning or automatically release a suspicious message. Admins explicitly release reviewed messages after required checks complete. Antivirus-positive content cannot be released. Rescan reruns the current policy and returns mail to quarantine. Previously received email is marked **Not screened · historical email**; it is not silently backfilled or falsely labelled safe.

## Sender authentication and forwarded mail

Whether a message authentically comes from its sender is decided by SPF, DKIM and DMARC, never by the AI and never by headers the message carries. For SMTP mail, Thura's own Rspamd scan evaluates them and Thura reads only Rspamd's result symbols (`R_SPF_*`, `R_DKIM_*`, `DMARC_*`); `Authentication-Results` headers that arrived with the message are ignored. The result is stored on the screening row and shown on the message's protection panel, for example **Sender verified · SPF pass · DKIM pass (example.com) · DMARC pass** or **Sender not verified · SPF fail · DKIM none · DMARC fail**. Verified SMIP deliveries show **Delivered over SMIP · sender verified**.

The sender is **verified** when DMARC passes, or SPF passes for an envelope sender aligned with the From domain, or a DKIM signature aligned with the From domain verifies. Alignment is relaxed: the same organizational (registrable) domain. A verified sender is still screened on content; authentication is not safety.

SPF needs the SMTP transaction. Managed Postfix's pipe transport passes the connecting client's IP, its HELO name and the envelope sender (`${client_address}`, `${client_helo}`, `${sender}`, with `null_sender=` so a bounce keeps its empty sender) to the relay adapter, which sends them in the signed `X-Thura-Envelope` header (see [Mail connectors](mail-connectors.md)). Thura stores them with the screening row and passes them to Rspamd as its `IP`, `Helo` and `From` request metadata, so a Rescan evaluates them again. A signed relay that reports no envelope, or locally generated mail without a client IP, shows SPF **not checked**: Thura never evaluates SPF against its own connection to Rspamd. DKIM and DMARC are still evaluated from the message. Without a configured Rspamd, the panel shows **Sender authentication not checked**. Rspamd does not run as a Postfix milter; Postfix adds no trusted `Authentication-Results` header, so there is no header to trust.

**Forwarded mail is judged by its forwarder**, the actual sender. The original author named inside forwarded content, its domain, and whether the forwarding chain is legitimate are deliberately out of scope: a forwarded alert from a colleague whose own domain passes SPF/DKIM is "sender verified", and the model is told that a difference between the forwarder's and the original sender's domain is not a risk signal. The forwarded content is still screened like any other content (credential or payment requests, deceptive links, dangerous attachments, urgency or deception in what it asks the reader to do).

Reputation, rate limits and unknown-sender approval keep their existing identity namespaces (`smip:` for verified SMIP, `unverified:` for everything else). They are computed synchronously at intake, before the asynchronous Rspamd scan, and SPF/DKIM/DMARC authenticate a **domain**, not an individual mailbox: any account at a shared provider passes DMARC for that provider's domain, so treating DMARC pass as "this exact contact" would let another user of the same domain bypass approval. Blocks and complaints already recorded therefore continue to apply unchanged.

### Trusted senders must authenticate

**Approve sender** trusts the address, not every message that claims it: the notice reads "Sender approved. Future mail from no-reply@example.com is trusted when it passes SPF/DKIM/DMARC." A trusted reputation satisfies the unknown-sender approval gate only for a message whose own sender is **verified** (SMIP, or the verified rule above). Otherwise the message is treated as an unknown sender:

- **At intake**, before the Rspamd scan, only SMIP proves the sender. Relay mail from a trusted address is never settled clean at intake; it stays held (pending) until the worker has its SPF/DKIM/DMARC result.
- **Not checked** (no Rspamd configured, or a scan that did not complete): the message is not known through trust and approval is required as for an unknown sender. A deployment without Rspamd therefore needs an administrator's approval on each relay message from a trusted sender when unknown-sender approval is on; approving the sender on a message approves that message.
- **Failed**: when the From address is trusted (under either identity) or a workspace member's address, and this message's checked result is not verified with a real verdict (a fail, an error, or a pass that is not aligned), screening adds the danger finding `trusted_sender_unverified`, "This sender is approved, but this message failed SPF/DKIM/DMARC — it may be forged", with risk 100, so it is quarantined and needs approval; Release refuses it as an unknown sender. A result in which every mechanism is only `none`/`not checked` is not verified (approval required) but is not called a forgery.

The same rule applies on Release and Mark safe. Trust never skips screening: Rspamd, antivirus, AI and the static checks run for trusted senders exactly as for anyone else.

**No-reply and automated senders are never exempt.** The no-reply / List-Unsubscribe / Auto-Submitted / Precedence rules (see [Real person label](#real-person-label)) answer only whether a person wrote the message; they skip none of Rspamd, antivirus, AI, scoring or approval.

## Scanning and failure handling

Static checks inspect sender display names and lookalike domains against a bounded directory (500 contacts plus 1,000 members), deceptive/obfuscated links, private/IP destinations, executable and macro names/content, active PDF content and ZIP metadata. ZIP inspection is bounded to 512 entries, 50 MiB expanded size and compression ratio 100. Links are **never fetched**, so screening cannot cause requests to attacker-selected/internal destinations. This is structural detection, not a remote URL reputation feed or browser sandbox. The existing sandboxed reader continues blocking external links, remote images and active HTML.

The private ClamAV service scans complete raw MIME, including attachment/archive/mail parsing and phishing signatures. Encrypted archives, scan limits and uninspectable content are treated as unsafe. No attachment bytes are sent to the LLM. Downloads, inline-image rendering and forwarding/sending copies check quarantine state; positive antivirus findings remain blocked even after human review. Existing already-downloaded copies cannot be recalled.

AI receives sender, subject, plain/HTML bodies, attachment names/types, static findings and the sender authentication result (method, From domain, SPF, DKIM with signing domain and alignment, DMARC, verified) as untrusted evidence. Its instructions take that result as the whole answer to sender authenticity and, for forwarded mail, limit it to the forwarder; it assesses only the message's own risk signals. It has no tools, link fetching or mail-sending capability. It must return a validated structured classification; malformed, refused, tool-calling, truncated or oversized responses are failures. Input is limited to 24,000 JSON bytes; larger email stays held for manual handling rather than silently classifying an excerpt. A benign AI verdict never lowers deterministic risk. Hosted providers receive this content automatically and may charge; choose local Ollama to retain inference locally. AI is an additional signal, not a guarantee against prompt injection, phishing or spam.

Mail with pending/unavailable required checks stays in Spam and its attachments remain inaccessible. Workers lease attempts and reconcile crashes every minute. Five failed attempts leave a visible error requiring repair and Rescan; no fake/safe fallback occurs. Settings revisions fence old worker results. Explicit reports/moves/release also fence in-flight results. Turning AI off affects future scan attempts, including queued jobs; use Rescan for previously held/error mail. Disabling AI does not disable static or configured antivirus checks.

## Real person label

Screening also decides whether inbound mail comes from a person or a machine, and tags people with the workspace's **Real person** system label (pinned under Mail's folders for every member until they unpin it). The decision is recorded on the screening row (`personState`, `personSource`, `personDetail`) and shown in the protection panel as "Real person" or "Automated: List-Unsubscribe header". It is made in this order:

1. **Headers decide first** (`mailabuse.AutomatedReason`, at intake): `List-Unsubscribe`, `Auto-Submitted` other than `no`, `Precedence: bulk|list|junk`, or a no-reply / do-not-reply / mailer-daemon / postmaster / bounces sender make the message automated. Nothing the model or the message says changes that; the model's sender answer is ignored for such mail.
2. **The model**, in the same screening call (no second call): its JSON adds `senderKind` (`person`, `automated` or `unsure`). The prompt tells it to judge the message's form and to ignore statements about who wrote it ("I am a real person"), since the email is untrusted.
3. **Fallback**, when there is no model (AI off, not ready, skipped after a local hold) or it answers `unsure` or `unknown`: the sender is a person if someone in the workspace has sent mail to that exact address (To, Cc or Bcc of any outgoing message) or it is a saved contact; otherwise "Not recognised as a real person".

Only mail that reached the Inbox carries the tag: spam and quarantined mail are classified but tagged when released, and Report spam, Block sender or Rescan remove the tag until screening releases the message again. Mail received before this feature (no `personState`) is never classified or backfilled. The label is created lazily per workspace (the first time a member's pins are read or a person's mail is tagged; a member-made label named "Real person" is adopted), cannot be deleted, and is unpinned per member with a stored `pinned=false` row, so the default pin needs no row per member.

## Deployment

The setup wizard exposes infrastructure defaults; existing workspace policy is changed in Mail settings. Keys follow the existing sealed configuration system and environment precedence:

| Setting | Default | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `MAIL_AI_SCREENING` | `true` | Initial AI choice for a new workspace policy; does not overwrite existing choices. |
| `MAIL_AI_PER_HOUR` | `120` | Maximum screening job reservations per workspace/UTC hour (1–10,000). Provider-level retries obey the configured LLM retry limit. Excess email waits until the next hour. |
| `MAIL_CLAMD_ADDRESS` | empty outside Compose | Private `host:port` ClamAV INSTREAM endpoint. Blank disables signature scanning, visibly; static inspection remains. |
| `THURA_AI_ENABLED`, `LLM_*` | existing AI configuration | Required enabled model. No model/fake production provider means required AI scans cannot complete. |

`deploy/compose.yaml` builds the nonroot `deploy/clamav` image and connects Thura to `clamav:3310`. No scanner host port is published. Its signature volume persists across upgrades. Allow FreshClam access to the official update service and allocate memory for the signature database (the acceptance container used 3 GiB). Initial signature-download failure prevents scanner startup and keeps mail held. Monitor FreshClam logs/signature freshness and scanner health; the application verifies scan completion but does not independently enforce signature age. Update images/signatures regularly. ClamAV is separately licensed GPL software in its own container; retain upstream notices/source obligations when distributing images.

A required service outage intentionally holds mail instead of delivering it unchecked. Configure the model in AI settings before enabling incoming traffic, or explicitly turn AI screening off for the workspace. Quarantine consumes private mail storage; ordinary retention and edge volume controls are still necessary. No spam/phishing-proof claim is made.

## Verification

`tests/mail_abuse_test.go` covers shared signed-relay/two-server SMIP intake, rate rollback and duplicate receipts, workspace boundaries, approval/complaints, AI opt-out/budget/failure, stale-worker fencing and antivirus/quarantined attachment enforcement. Unit tests cover structural deception, executable/ZIP/PDF detection and the ClamAV framing protocol. `e2e/mail-protection.spec.ts` checks actual desktop/phone settings and review screens. Existing mail reader, autosave and delivery suites remain intact.

For real local services, run `go run ./scripts/mail-abuse-acceptance -scanner 127.0.0.1:13310 -model gemma3:4b` against an operator-owned private ClamAV listener and installed Ollama model. It uses only synthetic content: harmless mail, the harmless EICAR antivirus test and a phishing fixture with an embedded instruction to classify it as safe. It never sends customer email.
